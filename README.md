# Meanwhile, in L.A.

A street atlas of cinematic Los Angeles and the real local places that stand in for other story settings. React, TypeScript, Vite, and MapLibre GL JS; hosted on GitHub Pages.

## Develop

```sh
npm ci
npm run dev
```

OpenFreeMap vector tiles supply the paper basemap, with OpenMapTiles/OpenStreetMap attribution. No API keys or backend are required.

## Interactions

- Search people, places, titles, and addresses; browse Places, People, or Routes. Title searches ignore periods, including `LA` / `L.A.`.
- Film titles show release years in the selector, directory, map labels/hover, photo captions and route pages; series show their original run. Selecting a film opens a compact movie panel with its verified IMDb link; location and route film tabs also have direct IMDb links. These release dates are separate from the canonical story timeline.
- Locations set elsewhere carry a Set outside L.A. tag in the directory, detail page and map label/hover. Fast Times’ fictional Ridgemont is tagged L.A. setting unconfirmed, without claiming it is definitively outside the county. L.A. stories only removes them from Places, People, search and the map; Show all restores them. The setting filter is preserved in shared links as `setting=la`.
- Filter by film, location types, deaths, or current map view. Layers contains independent Homes, Work, Hangouts, Events and Deaths checkboxes plus All locations; these affect the directory and map, survive movie changes and shared links, and allow combinations. Older single-type links still work. L.A. stories only is also in Layers. Spoilers stays beside the Layers button when its menu closes, retaining the existing local preference and redaction behavior. Movie tabs in place details and map labels immediately filter to that title and fit its mapped locations into view. The movie selector and a single Show all action stay visible while viewing filtered details. There are no separate × buttons for clearing movie or text filters. Show all also returns the map to the city view.
- Switch between Map and Satellite. Satellite uses Esri World Imagery with the atlas labels, location icons, routes, and county fog above it. Shared links retain the chosen view; Show all clears filters while preserving it.
- Layers also toggles Neighborhoods and Landmarks independently, both off by default. Neighborhoods adds named boundaries; Landmarks adds small blue-gray reference points with address, official-site and Google Maps popups. These reference layers stay independent of movie, person and story-date filters and do not add entries to the movie directory. Show all preserves them, as it preserves the basemap; copied links retain the toggles via `layers=neighborhoods,landmarks`. They load only when enabled, keep their sources while hidden, and remain beneath the county fog in both basemap modes.
- Danger heatmap in Layers adds an optional amber-to-red density overlay weighted by known fatalities, with a compact Low/High key. It follows the movie, canonical story-date and L.A.-setting filters; individual icon checkboxes, search, people and route browsing do not suppress it. Individually cataloged victims contribute one each; sourced group totals in `research/death-counts.json` replace that count without adding the named victims again. Uncertain totals use confirmed minimums. Unlocated scenes are omitted and approximate story pins retain their catalog placement. Shared venues contribute only the selected story’s deaths. Numeric counts are stored before spoiler redaction, so Spoilers does not alter the pattern. The render source contains only coordinates and numeric weights, with no names, titles or outcome text. Show all preserves the toggle and copied links retain `layers=danger`. `src/danger-layer.ts` uses a native map heatmap with a retained source, above satellite imagery and below labels, reference overlays and county fog; camera motion does not rebuild it.
- House prices in Layers adds compact red K/M callouts above visible homes with verified Zillow values, including clearly marked monthly apartment/house rents (`2.4K/mo`, `1.9K–2.3K/mo`). It defaults off and is preserved by Show all and shared links (`layers=prices`). Badges follow movie, person, search, story-date and setting filters, and hide when Homes is disabled. Hover shows the movie, place, actual filming-property address, full amount, purchase-value/rent distinction and check date; available-unit rents are labeled as unit listings, with source restrictions and concessions retained; selecting a badge opens the home’s details with the Zillow link and value. Linked homes without a reliable value show Price unavailable in their information pane while the overlay is enabled. Callouts avoid overlaps and retain their marker elements during camera movement. Both map styles and mobile are supported.
- Open each real filming address in Google Maps. Filming addresses are labeled separately when the story position differs.
- Open paired movie stills and real-site photographs, enlarge either, and expand Sources for credits and citations. Capture dates appear when verified; undated and archival photographs are retained without claiming they are current. Aerial photographs are explicitly labeled.
- Use the small arrows beneath a movie image to browse additional location views. Shared places retain separate frames and galleries for each film or series; screen thumbnails preserve the full frame, and image enlargement credits the frame currently shown.
- Verified home properties have direct Zillow links beside their filming-site Maps link. Building pages represent the whole building, with no invented apartment unit. Links refer to the real filming property, including when the fictional story uses another address.
- The compass follows map rotation; tap it to return north. Zooming out stops at a county overview fitted to the map’s current width, height, and rotation. Islands stay outside that overview until a researched location reveals them.
- Use My location to request browser geolocation and plot a position and accuracy circle. Coordinates are not saved in shared links or application storage. A secure origin and browser/OS permission are required.
- Spoilers is off by default. Map icons retain their category shapes and movie colors with spoilers hidden. Black marker strokes cover only revealing words and phrases in lists, detail pages, marker labels, hover tips, route stops, people associations and source metadata. Ordinary names, addresses, dates and surrounding prose stay readable; a name is covered only when it reveals a plot point. Death-event labels conceal the victim’s identity as well as the outcome, including hover/accessibility labels, route references, image metadata and citations. Protected death events do not appear as character-page associations or identity-only search matches; normal home entries remain readable. Revealing screen images remain covered. Individually reviewed ordinary views, including Gloria Revelle’s house, stay visible on normal location pages. Protected event pages still cover film images so a visible face cannot disclose a hidden participant. Each text and photo cover gets a separate generated ink outline and angle, stable across rerenders. Check Spoilers to restore the full content. The preference is saved locally, stays unchanged by Show all, and is never enabled by a shared link. Redacted stills cannot be enlarged. Browser checks include the ink edges in the directory, map hover tips and image covers at desktop and phone widths.
- Filter canonical story dates with a range, decade preset, or playback. Returning both endpoints to their limits restores All time.
- The map reaches the top of the window. Branding and Copy link live in the left directory pane; Spoilers stays beside Layers on the map. Phones open the directory over the full-height map.
- Copy link preserves selections and filters in short readable fragments such as `#eady-bosch-home?film=bosch`, `#route/bus-2525` and `#person/harry-bosch`. Old numeric links still open and are normalized to readable ones. Route/person context survives sharing a selected location, so Back returns to its originating page.
- Open a person’s page to see their locations. Locations preserve their originating person or route so Back returns to the same page.
- Routes includes sourced pursuits, walks and outings, with a compact type tag; it displays every matching route in a different movie color; route stops have location pages and photographs. Return visits to the same coordinate share a marker such as 1·3·5, while the ordered sidebar retains every visit. Route connectors link documented scenes, not verified continuous travel.
- Movie colors apply to location icons; routes have distinct colors. Labels appear on hover, keyboard focus, or selection; there are no always-on featured labels. Hovered and selected labels render above other location icons. Deaths appear as full-size, separately selectable entries in All and Deaths. With Spoilers enabled, death pages list the victims and people pages retain participants’ links to standalone death scenes; derived death entries do not misassociate surviving event participants. At shared venues, a death entry retains only the relevant film appearances, dates, people and film images; unrelated film/character searches do not return it. The Rialto’s David Kahane event belongs to The Player, while its La La Land cinema date remains a separate venue appearance.
- Map markers are reconciled by ID and survive camera movement. Place, death and route-stop icons use centered, zero-offset anchors with subpixel positioning, so zooming out does not move them away from their coordinates. Co-located records share that point; selecting a location raises its icon and label. Viewport reports update the directory only while In map view is checked. The fog reuses a georeferenced, overscanned texture during motion, settles to an exact mask afterward, skips uniform views, and paints boundary masks at half resolution with the same visible feather. Satellite mode hides the covered vector basemap.
- The territory outside the county is solid black with a feathered boundary. One canvas mask covers the basemap and its labels, so roads, place names, and imagery fade out together. It follows pan, zoom, and resize; both map modes share it. Islands remain masked until a researched place establishes one as part of the atlas.

## Data

`research/locations-100.json` preserves the initial 100-entry catalog: 96 places and four routes. Three unmatched production geocodes remain outside the prototype directory. The original directory contains 93 places; unresolved story addresses remain searchable without a false precise pin. `research/expansion-100.json` aggregates 100 further researched places from the three `expansion-*.json` source manifests. `route-revisions.json` and `route-extra.json` add eight further places for LAX and route stops. Heat’s robbery and meeting locations are supplemented in `heat-robberies.json` and `heat-lives.json`; `heat-routes.json` keeps the Cheritto branch of the bank escape. The ambulance theft, armored-truck robbery and Johnie’s Broiler are separate places without a connecting route. The T2 channel chase is one event at the documented bridge/gate site, not a route; its original gate photo pair is retained in the event’s Sources and component research. Old route links redirect to the corresponding event. `franks.json` records the diner’s eligible shared appearances and its rejected non-L.A. roles. `player-strange-days.json` and `speed-crash.json` add the requested films with story-first placements, paired photographs, and a segmented Speed bus route. `smoke-house.json` distinguishes Argo’s named Smoke House from La La Land’s Lipton’s exterior/interior composite; `voyager-locations.json` records the crew’s other 1996 city stops. `argo-locations.json` expands the Hollywood cover-film operation while excluding Los Angeles production sites that play Tehran or Washington; the film’s Burbank studio is distinguished from the historical Studio Six address. `live-die-chase.json` keeps supported downtown/river chase sections separate from an unlocated freeway sequence. `revisedPlaces` updates existing shared locations without duplicating the physical site.

Run `node scripts/prepare-data.mjs` after editing research. `scripts/enrich-data.mjs` applies story geography, dates, deaths, expansion manifests, route revisions, and photograph manifests to `src/data/atlas.json`. `research/geocodes.json` preserves production coordinates; `research/story-geography.json` records conflicting story evidence and its sources. Story positions take precedence. `research/story-map-corrections.json` replaces seven whole-street building highlights with location icons. Fictional address numbers can supply an approximate street interpolation; street-only references use a representative street point, explicitly marked approximate. Aunt Ruth’s point follows the screenplay’s Fountain-to-Sunset segment. These are display estimates, not identified parcels. Deckard’s apartment uses an explicitly labeled editorial downtown approximation; its canonical street remains unknown and Ennis House stays the separately labeled filming address. Broad inferences use dashed areas; unresolved city-only places have no pin. Uncontradicted filming locations remain proxies, rather than independently established canonical addresses. Dashed routes connect selected stops, not turn-by-turn paths.

`research/expansion2-100.json` aggregates another 100 distinct places (IDs 400–499) from the `expansion2-crime`, `expansion2-classic`, `expansion2-lives`, and `expansion2-tv` manifests, which brought the directory to 359 places in that batch. Its 12 TV additions are recurring landmarks: the original Baywatch headquarters and Mitch’s canal home, Rockford’s trailer, New Girl’s loft and bar, the three Modern Family homes, the Banks mansion, Fisher & Sons, Casa Walsh, and the Peach Pit. No episode-only TV stops are added. The Banks and Walsh homes follow their story addresses; the Peach Pit has a labeled western-L.A. estimate rather than a Pasadena pin. New Girl’s bar keeps its exterior and interior components together. Research audits preserve exclusions, uncertain placements, geocoder matches, and photo limitations. All additions have source-linked screen/site pairs; archive, undated, and aerial photographs are labeled accordingly.

`research/north-county.json` adds the original Agua Dulce ranch/park sites from Nope and the recurring Hammond home from Santa Clarita Diet. Universal’s later Jupiter’s Claim rebuild is separate from the original story site. Northern filming stand-ins with unsupported local story geography remain in the research audit. Craig and Gina Pomeroy’s recurring Baywatch loft is documented with official episode frames in `research/baywatch-craig.json`; its production address remains unverified, so it is a research candidate without a map pin.

The (500) Days of Summer bench is named Angels Knoll and can be found by searching Angels Flight, the neighboring railway. This does not assert a railway ride in that film.

Offscreen deaths are included when a cited source establishes them. `research/terminator-police.json` records the Central Division station attack and keeps the demolished Vernon production site in provenance only. Central Division / West Highland does not establish a story street address, so the station remains searchable with photographs and deaths but has no precise map pin.

`research/t2-arrival.json` adds the T-1000’s 1995 arrival beneath the old Sixth Street Viaduct near Santa Fe and Mesquit. Matched scene photographs and MovieMaps locate the scene area; the point is a filming proxy without an independently established story street address or surveyed portal footprint. The film frame is paired with a dated 2022 photograph of the replacement bridge. The patrol officer’s death is a separate, protected event; the ordinary arrival page does not identify a victim or describe the killing.

Dates establish presence during a story, not continuous tenancy. Explicit dates override release years. For otherwise contemporary stories, release year is an editorial estimate displayed with `c.`. Falling Down uses June 12, 1991 from the official Cannes synopsis; Blade Runner remains in November 2019. Future dates are not replaced by production years.

`research/media-a.json`, `media-b.json`, and `media-recent.json` record photograph sources, credits, capture dates when known, and component distinctions. Actual movie frames and local site JPEGs are under `public/photos`; `credits.json` preserves provenance. Some buildings have been demolished, and current-site photographs may show their replacements or demolition. A site's image can depict a production component rather than its story geography. When per-title photographs are available, movie and time filters select the corresponding frame. Shared sites retain an explicitly named screen image so it is not silently attributed to another film.

`public/la-county.geojson` is a simplified US Census TIGERweb county boundary, including islands; provenance is in `research/county-boundary.json`. `public/story-streets.geojson` preserves OpenStreetMap reference geometry, with its query recorded in `research/street-geometry.json`; the map no longer draws entire named roads for individual buildings.

`research/link-slugs.json` keeps permanent, neutral place and route slugs separate from changing display names. Add entries for new locations and death-event records without renaming existing slugs. `src/links.mjs` resolves the readable fragments, keeps legacy numeric bookmarks, and serializes navigation/filter context. Spoiler outcomes and hidden identities do not appear in place slugs.

`research/spoiler-audit.json` records exact spoiler phrases by place/route ID and field path, with separate fields for revealing screen images. Per-title selectors preserve innocent appearances at shared sites. There are no whole-record text redactions for ordinary places; death-only event labels conceal the entire identity/outcome label. Complete revealing clauses conceal who did what and how an event resolved, rather than leaving identities around a masked death/murder verb. Spoiler-only character associations are omitted, including hidden-only search matches. `concealParticipants` protects named escape/rescue/ending events locally without concealing those people’s ordinary homes. Reviewed safe image paths are listed in the audit; they do not override image protection on concealed event pages. Curated `deathVictims` entries cover named funerals and memorials without changing ordinary memorial landmarks or funeral-home premises. `src/spoilers.mjs` creates presentation copies; canonical facts, IDs and map geometry remain unchanged. Redacted text is replaced before rendering, including accessible labels, rather than hidden underneath CSS. Add new non-death reveals to the audit when extending the catalog.

`research/noncanonical-bttf.json`, `research/extra-halloween-valley.json`, `research/extra-cult-films.json`, `research/extra-lethal-weapon.json` and `research/extra-last-action-hero.json` add the expanded film catalog. `storySetting` records the named setting and whether it is canonically Los Angeles; uncertainty is explicit. Outside-setting pins use the actual local production site. Backlot-only locations remain excluded unless the studio lot is itself the story location. Doc’s Gamble House exterior and Blacker House interior are components of one home. The Orpheum represents New York’s Pandora interior. `mergeInto` joins new appearances at an existing public venue, retaining per-film photographs and placement notes instead of adding duplicate pins. `research/se7en-locations.json` adds the unnamed-city filming layer with protected reveal scenes; the desert records John Doe’s death only and Victor is not marked dead. `research/routes-expansion.json` records the additional sourced scene itineraries, preserves story/production address differences, and leaves unverified connecting streets untraced. `research/title-info.json` records verified IMDb IDs and release years, including the Earth Girls festival/U.S. release distinction; `src/films.mjs` formats these without changing title identities, movie colors or links.

The neighborhood reference layer is the Los Angeles Times’ historical 272-area [Mapping L.A. model](https://maps.latimes.com/neighborhoods/), including city neighborhoods, incorporated cities and county communities; it is not neighborhood-council geography or a set of current legal boundaries. [The publisher](https://maps.latimes.com/about/index.html) offers the simplified dataset under [CC BY-SA 2.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/2.0/). The public GeoJSON retains its attribution/license; properties and ring winding were normalized and one zero-area hole was omitted. Full provenance is in `research/neighborhood-overlay.json`. `public/la-landmarks.geojson` contains 45 independent mainland cultural/tourist references with official source links; `research/landmark-overlay.json` records each pin and address check. Neither reference dataset follows the fictional timeline or reveals unresearched islands.

`research/property-links-early.json` and `research/property-links-late.json` record direct Zillow matches and audited omissions for every home candidate. Demolished properties, approximate addresses and unverified pages do not get substitute links. `research/media-improvements.json` records additional film/TV frames and their original image/source URLs. Gallery frames retain individual spoiler flags. `research/property-prices.json` records checked Zillow snapshots and explicit omissions, backed by the three `property-prices-[a-c].json` research manifests, `property-prices-recheck.json` and the two `property-rents-[a-b].json` manifests. These are neither a live price feed nor historical values for the story year; source pages can predate the check date. Asking-price snapshots, Zestimates and monthly rents have distinct labels. Rental ranges describe available units in the matched building, without asserting which unit a character occupied; known concessions and eligibility restrictions remain visible. Tax assessments, old sale/rent prices, wrong-address listings, conflicting estimates and vacant-land asking prices are omitted. Linked filming-property values accompany the character’s existing story pin, including inferred placements such as Cher’s Beverly Hills address; the tooltip and information pane identify the real priced property, and price availability never moves the story location.

`research/eady-bosch-parcel.json` preserves the official county geometry for parcel 5556-015-004 at 1870 Blue Heights Drive. The county-mapped house footprint locates the pin within that parcel and replaces the interpolated point about 120 metres away; both story and production pins are corrected. The house's Maps action uses the full verified street address instead of coordinates.

`research/rocketeer-locations.json` adds three 1938 appearances: Griffith Observatory, the Rialto auditorium and Jenny Blake’s boarding house at the Mary Andrews Clark Home. Shared venues retain movie-specific images and do not inherit The Player’s Rialto death. The boarding-house pin is inside the official county building footprint; its site photo is explicitly undated. Miniatures, backlots, outside-county production sites and unresolved house attributions stay excluded.

`research/bosch-haunts.json`, `bosch-workplaces.json` and `bosch-events.json` add ten original-series locations: recurring bars and restaurants, Hollywood Station, LAPD headquarters, the county coroner, the Foltz courthouse and Angels Flight. Four join existing venues with separate Bosch photographs; Frolic Room has a verified site photo but no verified series frame, and never substitutes its L.A. Confidential frame under a Bosch filter. Red Studios’ replica interiors and book-only haunts are excluded. Named building/venue points replace street interpolations at Hollywood Station, Musso & Frank, Frolic Room and Angels Flight; the latter now uses its mapped upper ticket office. TV dates are approximate recurrence intervals, anchored to the final season’s New Year’s Eve 2019 opening and relative season gaps, rather than assuming the release year. The existing house remains the same shared Heat/Bosch property. Separately dated deaths use their own story descriptions and sources, appear only in their event year, and conceal the full revealing clause and victim identity when spoilers are off.

Bosch browser checks cover the expanded film directory, matching restaurant and station photographs, readable mobile venue names and addresses, hidden event identities and descriptions, opt-in restoration, and a keyboard timeline change from 2017 to 2018 that removes the deaths while retaining recurring venues.

Heatmap browser checks cover the Layers checkbox on/off state, rendering with every icon type hidden, Show all persistence, satellite imagery with neighborhood labels, anonymous spoiler-safe presentation, and a 390×844 phone viewport. The mobile legend remains above the Places control, and moving both timeline endpoints from 2017 to 2018 removes Bosch’s death hotspots without blanking the map. Device emulation was reset after checking. Automated checks validate fatality weighting, sourced group totals without double-counting, preserved weights with spoilers hidden, exclusion of unlocated massacres, film/date/setting filtering, source reuse and ordering in both basemaps, shared-link roundtrips and native renderer style validation.

Follow-up browser checks compare co-located event/death icons at close and wide zoom levels: both now remain centered on the same map point rather than separating by a fixed screen offset. The Nope filter also shows the sourced group event as a broader, hotter area than the lower-count ranch event, with spoilers still concealed.

`research/griffith-observatory-pin.json` records the Observatory’s official named-place map point and the replaced Census address interpolation, which was 578 metres north along a park road. The story and production coordinates, all film-specific views and Maps links use the Observatory building. La La Land’s separate “A Lovely Night” dance stays at Cathy’s Corner, over two kilometres north.

`research/soberin-pin.json` preserves the official County Westward Beach Road geometry and USGS elevation sample used to replace Soberin’s offshore marker with a land-based historical-site approximation. The vanished house’s exact footprint remains unverified; no parcel, road-shaped home or current house is asserted. `research/jane-blanche-beach-pin.json` similarly moves the neighboring Baby Jane scene from offshore to a sourced, approximate point on Westward Beach sand.

The timeline provides every decade from the 1930s through the 2020s alongside All time. Presets select canonical story ranges; the decade strip scrolls horizontally on narrow screens.
